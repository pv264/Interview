# Databricks Interview Questions & Behavioral Answers

## 1️⃣ “How do you handle an angry or frustrated customer?”

**🎯 What Databricks Tests:**
* Emotional control
* Ownership
* Communication
* Problem-solving under pressure

**✅ Interview-Ready Answer:**
“When a customer is frustrated, my first priority is to listen without interrupting and make sure they feel heard. I acknowledge the impact before jumping into technical details.
 and I will let them know I’m taking ownership of the investigation.
From there, I clearly explain what I’m checking, why I’m checking it, and what the next update will be.This helps build trust.

During the incident, I keep communication transparent and regular, and once the issue is resolved, I walk the customer through the root cause and preventive steps.
I’ve found that customers are much calmer when they see steady progress and clear communication, even during high-severity incidents.”

> **⭐ Senior Signal:**
> “I focus on reducing uncertainty for the customer, not just fixing the technical issue.”

## 2️⃣ “What do you know about Databricks and its customers?”

**🎯 What Databricks Tests:**
* Company understanding
* Role alignment
* Customer awareness (non-generic)

**✅ Interview-Ready Answer:**

Databricks customers typically deal with challenges around performance optimization, cluster scaling, job failures, cost management, and cloud integrations, especially as workloads grow in size and complexity.

What interests me most is helping customers move from reactive firefighting to stable, optimized, and cost-efficient platforms while clearly explaining complex issues in a way they can act on.”

> **⭐ Senior Signal:**
> “Most Databricks issues are multi-layer problems, not isolated bugs.”

## 3️⃣ “Give me a Linux troubleshooting example”

**🎯 What Databricks Tests:**
* Practical Linux knowledge
* Production mindset
* Calm troubleshooting

**✅ Interview-Ready Answer:**
“One common issue I’ve handled is a production system running out of disk space, which caused application failures and logging issues.
I started by checking overall disk usage to confirm the problem and then identified which directories were consuming the most space.
Instead of deleting files randomly, I traced the growth to excessive application and system logs. I safely cleared old logs, validated that services recovered, and then implemented log rotation and retention policies to prevent recurrence.
This approach restored service quickly while ensuring the issue didn’t repeat.”

> **⭐ Senior Signal:**
> “I focus on fixing the immediate issue while also preventing future incidents.”

## 4️⃣ “Give me an example where you used Python or Bash”

**🎯 What Databricks Tests:**
* Automation mindset
* Problem solving
* Practical scripting (not LeetCode)

**✅ Interview-Ready Answer:**
“I often use Bash and Python to reduce manual operational work.
One example was creating a small script that collected application logs, system metrics, and pod status information during incidents. Instead of manually gathering data from multiple commands, the script packaged everything in one place.
This significantly reduced investigation time and helped standardize troubleshooting, especially for escalated tickets.
I prefer writing simple, reliable scripts that improve response time and consistency rather than overly complex tooling.”

> **⭐ Senior Signal:**
> “Automation is about reliability and speed, not just reducing effort.”
